Jaguar
The jaguar is one of the key predators and is the final boss of the rainforest. It sits at the very top of the food chain, controlling populations of prey animals below it.
Harpy Eagle
Another key predator in the environment. It feeds on monkeys and sloths, attacking from the skies. As an apex flyer, it keeps populations of canopy animals in check.

Toucan
Toucans are a bird that relies on resources like small insects and fruits. They are secondary consumers feeding off primary consumers like the agouti and insects.
Pit Viper
A type of snake and a predator eating off the likes of organisms like birds and rodents. It ambushes prey on the forest floor and in the branches.

Agouti
A small group of rodents that take seeds and keep them to themselves. They are primary consumers eating directly off the producers, figs and nuts on the forest floor.
Tapir
A herbivore that eats off the fruits on the ground. It is a primary consumer browsing the forest floor, feeding directly on plant material produced by figs and nuts.
Spider Monkey
A type of monkey evolved to the rainforest environment, typically feeding off eggs, fruits, and insects. It bridges primary and secondary consumer roles in the canopy.
Only 1,000 kcal reaches this level. 9,000 kcal from producers was lost as metabolic heat before being passed up.

Figs
Figs are a primary producer as they serve as a resource for many animals. They capture energy from the sun through photosynthesis and form the base of the energy pyramid.
Nuts
Many animals that don't eat other organisms solely rely on plants and other non-meat resources. Nuts are one of these primary producers that anchor the rainforest food web.
All energy in this ecosystem originates here. Producers capture 10,000 kcal from sunlight, but only 10% gets passed to the next level.
